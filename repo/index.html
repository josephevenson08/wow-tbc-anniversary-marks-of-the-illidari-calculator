The findings

What a raid lead should act on before next week

  • 13×The top spender bought 13× as much as the bottom one. Costed item by item at live Anniversary prices, Thundaqt spent about 840g against Õhm's 65g. That gap is the whole argument for splitting Marks by spend rather than evenly — and it is wider than guessed prices suggested, because elixirs and food turned out to cost pennies while flasks, scrolls, runes and sappers do not.
  • 8Eight raiders used no scrolls at all. Scroll of Agility V and Strength V stack with flasks and every raid buff, cost a few silver, and last 30 minutes. Raid average is 52.7% — the cheapest fix on this sheet.
  • 5Five raiders ran an empty guardian slot on 40%+ of fights. Õhm had none for the entire raid; Likapally, Threatbull, Shoutofearth and Grazzyknoll sat at 35–59%. Free stats going unclaimed every pull.
  • 7thDonkin ranks 1st on preparation but 7th on gold spent. He drank the most Haste Potions in the raid (29, 238g of them) yet lands mid-table, because he covers his elixir slots with Elixir of Draenic Wisdom and Demonslaying at 3–4g a go while the raiders above him buy 77–92g flasks. Elixirs are roughly twenty times cheaper than flasks, so good prep and heavy spend are not the same thing — which is exactly why the split has a Score basis as well as a gold one.
  • 57Marks already spent on Shattrath flasks, by five raiders. Azzblast burned 17, Thotiana 16, Orixon 9, Pierogï 8, Donkin 7. They are converting Marks into flasks rather than gold — which is exactly what the reimbursement is for, and a reason their gold figure understates what the raid cost them.
  • 646Party haste applications delivered by six leatherworkers. Donkin alone accounts for 237 at 3.95 buffs per drum. Grazzyknoll and Splïff wasted 4 and 9 drums on Tinnitus — casts that hit zero targets.

Why the 22-point potion pool isn't split by type

A warrior can never drink a mana potion; a healer has no use for a Destruction Potion. Scoring them in separate buckets would hand every role a category it structurally cannot win. Pooling them measures the thing that is comparable across the roster: how many performance consumables you actually swallowed per fight, whatever your class drinks. Potions share a two-minute cooldown, so 2.0 per fight is a demanding but reachable bar — four raiders cleared it.

How gold spent is estimated

Counted consumables are simply quantity × unit price. Coverage percentages are converted to units first, using how long each buff lasts against a 3:24 clear: a flask runs two hours and survives death, so 100% flask coverage is charged as 2 flasks; elixirs run an hour and are lost on death, so 100% is 6; food and scrolls run 30 minutes, charged at 8 and 6; weapon enhancement at 6. Conjured items — Healthstones from the warlocks, a mage's Mana Emeralds — are priced at zero, because nobody bought them. Every price and unit count is editable in the panel above, since server economies differ by more than double.

Why Marks track spend rather than parse

A Mark of the Illidari buys a Shattrath flask outright, and the Phase 3 vendor trades one for ten Haste or Destruction Potions. That makes a Mark roughly 160g of consumable value at current potion prices, and it makes the distribution question simple: Marks are the raid paying back the people who funded the raid. Splitting them evenly means a raider who brought nothing gets the same rebate as one who spent 1,200g, which is the opposite of fair. The skew control lets you decide how hard to lean into that; the tank floor exists because tanking costs money whether or not the log shows a big potion count.

Is the order robust?

Every weight, cap and bonus ceiling was re-rolled 4,000 times at ±40% and the table re-sorted each time. 23 of 25 raiders land within two places of their listed rank in at least 86% of runs; Õhm finishes 25th in 100% of them. The top and bottom of this list are not artefacts of the weighting.

Where the 17 comes from

The analyzer reports coverage as percentages, not counts. Every distinct value on the sheet — 6, 24, 35, 41, 53, 59, 65, 71, 82, 88, 94 — is an exact seventeenth. Tested against denominators 15 through 19, 17 fits with roughly one-sixth the error of the next best, and the Illidan footnote listing fight IDs up to 80 agrees.

Read these before you post it

Where the prices come from

Every default on this page is a real TBC Anniversary auction price for the US region's Horde side, scraped from BootyBayBroker's hourly TradeSkillMaster feed on 6 September 2026 — 58 items, listed in full under the price panel. Blizzard's own Classic auction endpoints have returned 404 for the dynamic-classic1x namespaces since late 2024 and are still unfixed, so there is no first-party API to poll, and nothing realm-specific for Dreamscythe: these are region-wide. Prices move hourly. Edit any field, paste a list from your own Auctionator scan, or hit Restore live prices to come back to this snapshot.

A Mark is one redemption, not three

One Mark buys a Shattrath flask or ten Haste Potions or ten Destruction Potions — never a combination. At today's prices those are worth 92g, 82g and 82g, so which one you take changes what a Mark is worth. The toggle above sets it; Best takes the highest of the three. Note the flask is only the best deal for a caster — Relentless Assault at 77g is worth less than ten Haste Potions.

These numbers came from the log itself

Every buff on this page was read out of the report's own boss-only buff tables, one raider at a time, and cross-checked against the workbooks. The counts agree: Destruction Potions 20/21/23 for Pierogï, Azzblast and Kapisce, haste potions 29 for Donkin, 21 for Threatbull. The fight list confirms 17 boss encounters — 9 kills and 8 wipes.

Celions and the Windfury problem

6% weapon enhancement is the single biggest deduction on his card, but Windfury Totem takes the same imbue slot as a sharpening stone, so a ret paladin in the enhancement shamans' party physically cannot keep one up. Drop that category and he scores 72.2 and ranks 17th. Check the party layout before treating it as a prep failure.

Conjured items are free, and priced that way

Healthstones and Mana Emeralds cost the user nothing, so they earn score but no gold. That is deliberate — Azzblast's 37 Mana Emeralds shouldn't buy him Marks that Kapisce paid cash for. The warlocks who supplied the stones aren't credited for them either.

Missing combatant info

The analyzer warns that some Tier 6 fights lose the combatantInfo block carrying consumable data when a logger is far from the boss at pull. A raider at 94% rather than 100% may be a logging artefact rather than a missed flask.

Bosses only, Shahraz partly excluded

Trash is excluded from everything here. The gear audit additionally skips Mother Shahraz so shadow resistance sets don't register as errors — but her fight still counts toward consumable coverage.

Scroll data source

Scroll coverage is the analyzer's own per-fight pass over this log, including which scroll types and where a rank below V was used. Warcraft Logs itself sits behind a human-verification check, so nothing here was re-scraped from the site directly.
